FlySky i6X transmitter setup (firmware 1.0, 09-Feb-2022)

Goal: set up the i6X itself for our plan: correct model type, Mode 2 sticks, no transmitter mixing (ArduPlane does all mixing), SWA → CH5, SWC → CH6, receiver output mode (PWM in your setup, because of the PPM encoder). Then check every control on the transmitter's own Display screen, before involving the Pixhawk at all.

Safety: USB only, no LiPo, no propellers. The receiver is powered by the Pixhawk (USB) through RC IN.

Step 1: Start-up

1

Power on correctly

  1. All 4 switches UP, left stick (throttle) fully DOWN, then power on. Otherwise the i6X beeps a warning (Guide 04).
  2. Plug the Pixhawk USB in (powers the receiver). Receiver LED solid.
  3. Main screen: next to RX you should see a battery bar. The manual says: "If a receiver is not connected or bound to the transmitter no battery status will be displayed for the receiver." RX bar visible = linked ✅.

Step 4: Clean channel outputs (4 small checks)

Key rule on these screens (official manual §5.2–5.6): short OK = go to the next channel. UP/DOWN = change the value of the selected channel. So don't use UP/DOWN to scroll, because it changes things. CANCEL once = leave without saving · hold CANCEL 2 s = save and leave.

Open the list (same for 4.1–4.3): home screen → hold OK 2 s → highlight Functions setup (UP/DOWN) → short OK. The list order is: 1 Reverse · 2 End points · 3 Display · 4 Aux. channels · 5 Subtrim · 6 Dual rate/exp. · …

4.1

Reverse: all channels "Nor"

  1. In the list, Reverse is the 1st item → short OK.
  2. You see Ch1, Ch2… each with Nor or Rev. Press short OK to step through every channel. Just read them.
  3. All Nor → press CANCEL once. One shows Rev → while it's selected press UP or DOWN once (becomes Nor) → hold CANCEL.

Meaning: Rev flips a channel's direction. Direction fixes are done in ArduPlane later, never here.

4.2

End points: all channels 100% / 100%

  1. In the list, DOWN once to End points (2nd item) → short OK.
  2. Each channel shows two numbers: left = low end, right = high end. Short OK steps to the next channel.
  3. All 100% both sides → CANCEL once. Not 100 → select that channel, move its stick toward the side to fix (picks left/right box), UP/DOWN to 100 → hold CANCEL.

Meaning: end points limit how far a channel goes. ArduPlane needs the full range to calibrate.

4.3

Subtrim: all channels 0 (centre)

  1. In the list, DOWN to Subtrim (5th item) → short OK.
  2. Short OK steps through the channels. Each should show 0 / the marker in the middle.
  3. All 0 → CANCEL once. Not 0 → select it, UP/DOWN to 0 → hold CANCEL.

Tip: the highlighted channel's arrow can look ~1 pixel off. Press OK to move the highlight away and look again.

Meaning: subtrim shifts a channel's centre. It must be centred so "stick in the middle" really is the middle.

4.4

Trims: the 4 small sliders, centred (no menu)

Each bar has a fixed centre tick and a moving marker. Centred = the marker sits on the centre tick.

  1. Go back to the home screen (CANCEL until you're there).
  2. The 4 trim rockers sit next to the sticks: Trim 1 beside the left stick (vertical), Trim 2 below it (horizontal), Trim 3 beside the right stick, Trim 4 below it.
  3. Beep test per rocker: click once one way, then once back. A higher beep on the way back means centred ✅. Otherwise keep clicking toward the middle until the higher beep. The manual: "When the trim position reaches the middle, the transmitter beeps in a higher tone."
  4. Saved automatically.
✅ Step 4 done (18:31): Reverse all Nor · End points all 100%/100% · Subtrim all centred · Trims all centred. Nothing changed.

Meaning: trims shift the stick centre. ArduPlane handles trim itself, so they must be centred before calibration.

Step 7: Test on the transmitter itself

7

Functions setup → Display

From the home screen: hold OK 2 s → highlight Functions setup → short OK → DOWN 2× to Display (3rd item) → short OK.

This screen shows what the transmitter sends, live. No buttons to press here: you only move the sticks and switches with your fingers and watch the bars.

What it looks like: a list of bars (Ch1, Ch2, Ch3…). Each bar has a filled part that grows or shrinks as you move a control. Control centred = bar half full. Control at one end = bar empty, other end = full.

Where SWA and SWC are: top edge of the transmitter. SWA = left side, outer switch (2 positions). SWC = right side, inner switch (3 positions: up / middle / down).

Do one control at a time, slowly, end to end, and watch which bar moves:

MoveBar that must move
Right stick ←→Ch1 (roll)
Right stick ↑↓Ch2 (pitch)
Left stick ↑↓Ch3 (throttle)
Left stick ←→Ch4 (yaw)
SWA up/downCh5: one end ↔ other end
SWC up/middle/downCh6: end / middle / end

Short CANCEL to exit. (Don't hold OK there: that starts "scrub mode", which sweeps all channels.) 📸 Photo the Display with SWC in the middle.

All bars move correctly = the transmitter is set up ✅. If these move but the Pixhawk's Raw Channel Monitor doesn't, the problem is the receiver → Pixhawk cable: do Guide 04, Part C5 . In your case the fix was option B: i6X Output mode PWM so the PPM encoder works. Then the Radio calibration.
