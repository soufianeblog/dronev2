Board orientation + accelerometer & level calibration

Goal: tell ArduPlane which way the Pixhawk faces in the plane, then calibrate the accelerometer (6 positions) and the level. The compass comes last, outdoors. These remove two PreArm errors ("Compass not calibrated" and later "AHRS…") and are required before any flight-mode or motor test.

  • ⏳ E compass: still "PreArm: Compass not calibrated". Moved to after the wiring (Guide 06), because power cables and motors change the magnetic field. Do it outdoors.
  • ✅ F backup: params_after-guide03.params (03:32), params_after-guide03_2.params (03:54).
Safety for this guide
  • USB only. No LiPo. No propellers. You'll be turning the aircraft in every direction.
  • Turning the plane upside down with a USB cable attached: use a long cable, or have a helper hold the cable. Don't let the cable tug the Pixhawk.
🔁 What "reboot" means in these guides (added rev 2)

Reboot = restart the Pixhawk (not the Mac, not QGC). ArduPilot reads orientation and calibration settings only when it starts up, so after changing them the board must restart to use them. QGC itself says: "YOU MUST REBOOT YOUR VEHICLE AFTER EACH CALIBRATION."

How (pick one):

  1. Button: after a calibration, QGC shows a "Reboot Vehicle" button on the Sensors page. Click it. Or anytime: Vehicle Configuration → Parameters → Tools (top-right) → Reboot Vehicle → Ok.
  2. Cable: unplug the USB from the Pixhawk, wait 5 seconds, plug it back in.

Either way: don't move the plane for ~30 seconds while it starts (gyro calibration, "Do not move plane"). QGC reconnects by itself; the top bar shows "Not Ready" again.

B

Set "Autopilot Rotation" to None, then reboot

On the Sensors page you see a column of buttons on the left side of the page (to the right of the main Vehicle Configuration list): Accelerometer · Compass · Level Horizon · Pressure (Baro/Airspeed) · Sensor Settings (and maybe CompassMot). (Correction: rev 1 also listed "Gyro". QGC 5.1.5 hides that button for planes.)
  2. Click Sensor Settings. You see "Autopilot Rotation:" with the hint "If mounted in the direction of flight, select None."

Part C: Accelerometer calibration (6 positions)

C

Calibrate Accelerometer

  1. Where: purple Q icon → Vehicle Configuration → in the big left list click Sensors → on the Sensors page, the first button in the column of buttons is Accelerometer. Click it. A dialog shows "Autopilot Rotation:" again. It must say None now. Leave "Simple accelerometer calibration" unticked (it's less precise and only meant for vehicles too big to turn).
  2. Click OK. QGC shows 6 pictures. For each one: put the plane in that position, hold it completely still, then press Next. QGC's own text: "Hold still in the current orientation and press Next when ready."
#Position (ArduPilot order)Tip for a plane
1LevelOn the table, wheels/belly down
2Right side downRight wingtip down, hold the plane by the fuselage
3Left side downLeft wingtip down
4Nose downNose pointing at the floor
5Nose upTail on the floor
6On its backUpside down, cushion under the wing

Follow the order QGC shows if it differs. ArduPilot docs: "being still matters more than exact angles". Roughly 90° is fine, but don't move while it measures.

You should see: each picture turns green, then "Calibration complete" (or "Successfully completed"). QGC says: "YOU MUST REBOOT YOUR VEHICLE AFTER EACH CALIBRATION", so click the Reboot Vehicle button that appears on the Sensors page (or unplug/replug USB, see Reboot). Wait 30 s without moving the plane. 📸 Screenshot the result.

Failed? Usually movement during measuring. Just redo it.

Part D: Level Horizon

D

Level Horizon (plane in its level flying attitude)

  1. Place the plane in its level flying attitude: wings level, fuselage as it is in straight level flight. (Use a phone level app on the wing root if you like.)
  2. Where: Vehicle Configuration → Sensors → button Level Horizon (third button in the column) → OK. QGC: "To level the horizon you need to place the vehicle in its level flight position and press OK."
  3. Wait for "Calibration complete", then reboot the Pixhawk: click Reboot Vehicle on the Sensors page, or unplug/replug the USB. Wait 30 s.
What "level" means exactly (asked 03:27): yes, the plane sitting normally on a flat table, if it sits flat there. Check 2 things:
  1. Wings level left/right: neither wingtip lower than the other.
  2. Fuselage horizontal front/back: if the Y3 sits on landing gear or skids that tilt it nose-up or nose-down, put a book/foam under the low end until the top of the fuselage is horizontal.
Easy check: put your phone (level/measure app) on the top of the fuselage (front-back) and on the wing root (left-right). Both should read about 0°. A degree or two off is fine.
You should see: on the Fly view, the artificial horizon shows level when the plane is level. ArduPilot note: Level can only correct up to ~10°, so the board must already be mounted flat.

Bench path (situation 3): redo this step after installing the board in the plane.

For C and D: "reboot" does NOT mean restarting the app. QGroundControl stays open. You restart only the Pixhawk: click the Reboot Vehicle button QGC shows after the calibration, or unplug/replug the Pixhawk's USB cable. Wait 30 s without moving the plane.

Part E: Compass. Later, outdoors

Don't do this one at your desk. ArduPilot: "Do not calibrate the compasses near any metallic or magnetic field producing object", and have a good 3D GPS lock first, ideally outdoors. It must also be done with the board and GPS in their final position in the plane.

When you're ready (plane assembled, outside, GPS fix, USB to a laptop):

  1. Sensors → Compass → OK. QGC: "Rotate the vehicle randomly around all axes until the progress bar fills all the way to the right."
  2. Hold the plane in the air and point each side down to the ground in turn (nose, tail, left, right, top, bottom), a few seconds each, slowly turning.
  3. Success: bars full, "Successfully completed" (the buzzer plays rising tones). Then reboot the Pixhawk.
  4. Failed after several tries? Don't force it. Send me a screenshot (ArduPilot suggests relaxing "fitness" or COMPASS_OFFS_MAX, but only if needed).
