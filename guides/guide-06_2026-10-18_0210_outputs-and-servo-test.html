Outputs: plug in and test the servos (then the motors)

Connect the 6 servos and 3 ESCs to the right Pixhawk outputs, power the servo rail, and check that every surface and tilt servo moves the right way. Servos first. Motors come at the end of this guide, props off.

Safety, for this whole guide: propellers OFF on all 3 motors. The LiPo is only connected when a step says so. Keep fingers away from the tilt mounts when the servos get power: they can snap to an end position.

Your guess was right: the 2 tilt servos go to AUX 1 (front left) and AUX 2 (front right). Left/right always means as seen sitting in the plane, looking forward.

Step 1: Plug in the servos (Pixhawk unplugged)

1
  1. Unplug the USB and the LiPo, so the Pixhawk is off.
  2. Each servo plug has 3 wires: brown/black = −, red = +, orange/yellow/white = signal.
  3. On the Pixhawk pin row, the signal pin is the top row (the side with the labels "MAIN OUT" / "AUX OUT"), + in the middle, − at the bottom (next to the board's base). So: orange/yellow on top, brown/black at the bottom.
  4. Plug in following the table: MAIN 1 left aileron, MAIN 2 right aileron, MAIN 3 left tail, MAIN 4 right tail, AUX 1 left tilt, AUX 2 right tilt.
  5. Leave the ESC plugs out for now.

📸 Photo of the pin row with the plugs in (so the order and the orientation can be checked).

Step 2: Safe limits for the tilt servos (before they get power)

2

The old setup sends the tilt servos very far (AUX 1: 800–1940 µs, AUX 2: 700–2100 µs). Most servos stop around 900–2100 µs, and a tilt mount can hit its stops and strain the servo. For the first test we narrow them, then widen step by step once we see where the mount stops.

  1. USB in, wait 30 s, Parameters → Tools → Refresh.
  2. Set, one at a time (search, click, type, Save):
    ParameterNowSet to (temporary)
    SERVO9_MIN8001100
    SERVO9_MAX19401900
    SERVO10_MIN7001100
    SERVO10_MAX21001900

Step 3: Power the servo rail

3

The Pixhawk does not power the servo pins from USB or from the power module. The servos need 5 V on the rail (the middle + row) from a separate source. Two options with your parts:

OptionHowNotes
A. One ESC's BECIf your ESC signal plugs have 3 wires (with a red one), that red wire carries 5 V. Plug the tail ESC (MAIN 8) in: it powers the rail when the LiPo is connected.Use only one ESC's red wire. The other two ESC plugs must have their red wire lifted out of the plug (tape it back), so BECs don't fight each other.
B. PDB 5 V BECA lead from the PDB's 5 V pads to any free + / − pins on the rail (for example MAIN 7's + and −, signal pin empty).⚠️ Never the PDB's 12 V output: 12 V destroys the servos and can damage the Pixhawk.

Six metal-gear servos can draw 2–3 A when moving together. For flying, a dedicated 5–6 V UBEC of 3 A or more is the safest. For the bench test, A or B is fine.

Step 4: Test the wing and tail servos (FBWA)

4
  1. Props off. Transmitter on, switches up, throttle down.
  2. USB in, then the LiPo (to power the rail).
  3. Press and hold the safety button (on the GPS mast or separate) until its light is solid. This only allows the outputs to move; it doesn't arm anything.
  4. SWC down (FBWA).
  5. QGC → Vehicle Configuration → Servo Outputs: each row shows the live Position of one output.
  6. Move the sticks and compare with the table (and the drawings):
    MoveMust happen
    Right stick → rightLeft aileron down, right aileron up
    Right stick ↓ (pulled back)Both tail wings up
    Left stick → rightRight tail wing up, left tail wing down
  7. A surface moves the wrong way? On Servo Outputs, tick Reversed on that output's row, and test again.
Also tilt the plane by hand in FBWA: the surfaces push back toward level. Right wing down → right aileron goes down, left aileron goes up. Nose down → both tail wings go up.

Step 5: Test the tilt servos

5
  1. SWC middle (QHOVER): both front motors must point straight up.
  2. SWC down (FBWA): both tilt forward slowly (about 25°/s).
  3. Back to middle: they tilt back up (faster, about 80°/s).
  4. Watch for buzzing at an end position: that means the servo pushes against a stop. Tell me which motor and which end.
